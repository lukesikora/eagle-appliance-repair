Gas Range Repair vs Electric: Which Breaks More Often?

July 24, 2026 · Eagle Appliance Repair
Quick answer: Gas ranges fail more often due to igniter and burner issues, costing $150–$400 per repair. Electric ranges have fewer moving parts but heating element failures run $200–$500. Both need regular maintenance to avoid costly breakdowns.

If you're a West Jordan homeowner deciding between gas and electric cooking, or you already own one and wondering why the repair bills keep coming, this comparison matters. Both range types have genuine strengths and real weak points. Understanding which breaks more often—and why—helps you make smarter maintenance decisions and budget for the inevitable repair call.

Gas Ranges: High Failure Rates in the Kitchen

Gas ranges have more mechanical complexity than their electric counterparts. The igniter, burner caps, and control valves all work together to produce a flame, and when one component fails, cooking stops. Eagle Appliance Repair has replaced countless gas igniters in West Jordan homes because they're the single most common failure point.

An igniter typically lasts 3 to 5 years under normal use. When it fails, you'll hear clicking but see no flame. Replacement costs run $150–$300 depending on the model. Burner caps and grates also wear faster because they sit directly over flame and absorb more heat stress than electric coil elements. Expect to budget for cap replacement every 5 to 7 years at $40–$100 per set.

The control valve regulates gas flow to each burner. If it sticks or fails, you lose independent control of that burner's heat. Valve replacement is more involved and typically costs $250–$400. Over a 10-year ownership span, gas range owners commonly face 2 to 3 service calls for these issues alone.

Electric Ranges: Fewer Parts, Different Problems

Electric ranges have a simpler mechanical design. There's no igniter, no burner valve, no complex gas regulation. The heating coils plug directly into the stovetop, and most controls are electrical switches. This simplicity means fewer parts to fail—but the parts that do fail tend to be expensive.

The heating element itself is the vulnerable spot. Coils wear out from thermal cycling (heating and cooling repeatedly) over 8 to 10 years. When a coil burns out, the burner won't heat at all. Replacement costs $200–$350 per element, and some electric ranges have four separate coils, so multiple failures aren't uncommon in older units.

The oven's heating element (usually a 240-volt unit) is even pricier. A failed oven element runs $200–$500 to replace, plus labor. Control boards that manage the heating cycle can also fail, though this is less common. Electric range owners typically see fewer overall service calls than gas owners, but individual repairs often cost more.

Maintenance Costs: Gas Wins Short-Term, Electric Longer-Term

Annual preventive maintenance for a gas range includes cleaning burner caps and checking igniter spark. These tasks keep most issues at bay and cost little to nothing if you do them yourself. If you call a pro for an inspection and light cleaning, budget $75–$125 annually.

Electric ranges need far less routine maintenance. Keeping coils clean and dry is the main task. Many homeowners never call for preventive service on an electric unit—they wait until something fails. However, when it does fail, the single replacement often exceeds the cost of multiple gas range repairs.

Over 15 years, a gas range typically accumulates $600–$1,200 in repair costs. An electric range might see $400–$800 in total repairs, but one bad element failure can consume half that budget in a single service call. The math favors electric if you value predictability, but gas is cheaper if repairs spread evenly and you stay on top of maintenance.

West Jordan's Climate and Range Performance

West Jordan sits at roughly 4,200 feet elevation with dry, low-humidity summers and cold winters. This environment actually affects appliance performance in subtle ways. The low humidity is excellent for both gas and electric ranges—moisture doesn't corrode igniter contacts or short out electrical controls as easily as in humid climates.

However, hard water in the Jordan Valley area can affect water-using components. If you have a gas range with a water-fed igniter or any range with a convection fan that pulls in dry air, mineral buildup happens faster here than in softer-water regions. Homes near Redwood Memorial Golf Course or in the Oquirrh neighborhood sometimes report heavier dust accumulation during spring, which clogs burner ports and coil crevices. More frequent cleaning pays off.

Winter heating cycles are intense in West Jordan, and the constant on-and-off thermal stress on heating elements is real. Electric range owners in neighborhoods like Valley View or near Westpointe should inspect coils annually, especially if they use the oven frequently during cold months.

Common Repair Mistakes That Make Costs Worse

Many homeowners try to clean gas burners with water or harsh chemicals, which can damage igniter wiring. The igniter is sealed electronics—moisture inside means replacement is your only option. A quick call to Eagle Appliance Repair before you troubleshoot saves hundreds.

With electric ranges, people sometimes attempt to replace coils themselves. While the coils themselves are straightforward to swap, improper installation or using the wrong wattage element causes electrical problems that require a licensed tech to diagnose. That diagnosis call often costs $100–$150 on top of the element cost.

Ignoring pilot light issues on some gas ranges is another trap. A weak pilot can mean the main igniter works harder and fails sooner. Addressing pilot problems at year 2 or 3 prevents igniter failure at year 4. Early intervention keeps your total cost low.

Which Type Should You Choose?

If you value cooking control and lower repair costs spread over time, gas is the answer. If you prefer simplicity and are willing to pay more per repair when it happens, electric works fine. Neither type is inherently unreliable—both last 15 to 20 years with normal care.

For West Jordan residents, the choice often comes down to existing hookups and personal preference. Homes in South Jordan and Taylorsville frequently have gas lines already installed, making gas ranges cheaper to own initially. If your home is all-electric (common in newer construction near Riverton), electric ranges are your path of least resistance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does gas range repair typically cost near West Jordan, UT?

Igniter replacement runs $150–$300, burner valve work costs $250–$400, and cap replacement is $40–$100. Most single repairs fall between $150 and $350 once labor is included. Emergency same-day service may add a trip fee.

What's the average cost to replace an electric range heating element?

A single coil element costs $200–$350 to replace, while an oven element runs $200–$500 due to higher wattage and complexity. If your range has multiple failed elements, expect repair costs to climb quickly.

How often should I service my gas range?

Clean burner caps and check igniter spark every 6 months. Call a professional annually if you use the range heavily, or every 2 years for moderate use. Catching small issues prevents expensive failures.

Does West Jordan's water quality affect range repairs?

The Jordan Valley's mineral-rich water can cause buildup on gas igniter contacts and inside convection fans. Rinse burners with distilled water occasionally and have your range inspected if you notice hard water stains around the stovetop area.
