Signs Your Dryer Needs Professional Repair Now

Don't wait if your dryer isn't heating at all, makes grinding or squealing sounds, or shuts off mid-cycle. These point to worn components or electrical faults that won't fix themselves. Continuing to run a broken dryer can damage the drum, blower wheel, or wiring and compound the repair cost.

Clothes that come out damp after a full cycle are a red flag. The drum might be turning fine, but the heating element, thermostat, or vent blockage prevents moisture removal. That situation demands a technician inspection—attempting DIY fixes on internal heating systems creates fire hazards.

Lint buildup in the vent can feel like a repair, but it's actually preventative maintenance. If you notice clothes take two cycles to dry, have the vent professionally cleaned before it damages the heating element or motor.

Common Dryer Repair Mistakes That Cost More

Ignoring a thermal fuse failure and replacing it yourself might save $100 initially, but improper installation can blow the new fuse immediately or damage the control board. Professional installation ensures proper seating and continuity testing.

Another costly mistake: running the dryer on high heat when the drum rollers are worn. You'll hear squeaking or scraping, but pushing through "to save time" accelerates drum damage. That turns a $350 bearing repair into a $600+ drum replacement.

Never duct-tape or splice dryer vents to "temporarily" fix airflow issues. Restricted airflow overheats the motor and heating element, which can trigger thermal shutoffs or short-circuit the control board. Proper venting takes 30 minutes to inspect and costs far less than board replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I repair my dryer or replace it?

If your dryer is under 8 years old and the repair cost is less than half the price of a new machine, repair makes sense. Dryers over 12 years old with multiple failed parts might warrant replacement, especially if you have an energy-efficient model available. A technician can assess whether your machine is worth fixing during the diagnostic visit.

Can I fix a dryer vent blockage myself?

You can remove lint from the trap yourself, but professional vent cleaning requires a special brush kit and access to your exterior vent termination. Partial clogs hidden inside the wall create fire hazards. Have it inspected by a professional at least annually.

What's covered under appliance repair warranties?

Parts warranties typically cover the replacement component itself—like a heating element or drum roller—for 12 months. Labor is usually not covered after the initial repair date. Read the warranty document to confirm what's protected before you sign off on the work order.

How do I prevent future dryer repairs?

Clean the lint trap before every load, inspect the exterior vent quarterly, and avoid overloading the drum. Have a professional check the vent system annually and replace any damaged hose or ductwork. These simple steps extend your dryer's lifespan by years.
