Common Refrigerator Problems in Cottonwood Heights Homes

Cottonwood Heights sits at about 5,200 feet elevation with significant temperature swings between seasons. This climate puts particular stress on refrigerator components. Hard water from local supply lines also accelerates mineral buildup in icemakers and water dispensers.

The most frequent issues we encounter include icemakers that jam or stop producing ice, water dispensers that leak or deliver slow trickles, compressors that cycle constantly or fail to run, and doors that don't seal properly because hinges have loosened or gaskets have deteriorated. Condensation pooling on shelves often signals a clogged defrost drain, a relatively inexpensive fix if caught early.

Refrigerators in this area also suffer from dust accumulation in condenser coils due to the dry Utah climate. When coils become caked with lint and dust, the compressor has to work harder, energy bills spike, and failure becomes likely within months.

When to Call for Emergency Service vs. Waiting for a Standard Appointment

Emergency same-day service makes sense if your fridge has completely stopped cooling, is leaking water inside the home, makes alarming grinding or clicking sounds, or smells like burning plastic. These situations warrant immediate attention.
