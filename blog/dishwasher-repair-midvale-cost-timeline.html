Common Dishwasher Failures We See in Midvale Homes

The spray arm—the rotating piece at the bottom that shoots water upward—clogs more often than any other part. Hard water deposits build up inside the little holes, restricting water flow. Your dishes come out dirty or spotted. A simple cleaning sometimes works; replacement runs $80–$150.

Door seals wear out around year 8–12. You'll notice water pooling at the base during a cycle or detecting a musty smell inside the tub. Replacing a seal takes 45 minutes and costs $60–$120 in parts.

Control boards fail when moisture gets inside the electrical housing, especially in kitchens with poor ventilation. The dishwasher won't start, cycles randomly, or shuts off mid-wash. This is a $250–$350 repair once labor is included.

Drain clogs are the fourth most common issue. They're also the trickiest to diagnose. Is it a clogged filter screen? A kinked hose under the sink? A blocked drain line inside the unit? A technician needs to visually inspect and may need 1–2 hours to clear it.

DIY vs. Professional Repair: Where the Line Is

You can safely clean the spray arm holes with a thin wire or toothpick and soak the filter basket in vinegar. You can also check the door gasket for visible cracks or food debris and clean it yourself. These tasks take 20 minutes and cost nothing.

Do not attempt to replace the spray arm yourself if you're unfamiliar with appliance repair. You'll need to remove the lower wash arm, unclip it from its center post, and install the new one—simple in theory, but misalignment can cause leaks or water damage. A technician handles it correctly the first time.

Never open the control panel or touch electrical components. Dishwashers run on 120 volts and capacitors hold residual charge even when unplugged. One wrong move means injury or a fried circuit board that costs more to replace than calling us in the first place.

Midvale's Water Quality and Your Dishwasher

Midvale sits in Utah's hard water belt. Your tap water carries dissolved minerals—primarily calcium and magnesium—that build up inside appliances over time. Dishwashers suffer from this more than most because they run multiple cycles weekly and maintain standing water during storage.

Homes in the Meadow Hills and Fort Canyon areas of Midvale experience some of the highest mineral content in the valley. You'll see white scale inside the tub, clogged spray arm holes, and accelerated wear on seals. Using a rinse aid and running a monthly vinegar cycle (empty load with a cup of white vinegar) slows the buildup.

If you're getting repair calls more than once every 18 months, it's worth installing a whole-house water softener. It saves money on appliance repairs and extends the life of your unit by 3–5 years.
