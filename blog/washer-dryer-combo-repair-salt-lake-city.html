Why Combo Units Cost More to Repair Than Separate Machines

A standalone washer and dryer each have their own motor, pump, drum, and control board. A combo unit crams all those components into one chassis, where they're tightly integrated. When one part fails, technicians often need to disassemble much more of the machine to access it.

Take the drum seal, for example. On a traditional washing machine, replacing it takes about an hour. On many combo units, technicians must first remove the drum assembly, then access the seal behind a sealed chamber. That same job stretches to 2-3 hours, doubling labor costs.

Parts are also harder to source. Manufacturers don't always make combo replacement components available separately. Instead, technicians order complete assemblies—like an entire control module—when only a single circuit board failed. A $40 part becomes a $150 assembly.

Common Combo Unit Failure Points and Repair Costs

The thermal sensor is the first thing to fail on most combo units we service in Salt Lake City. This sensor tells the machine when clothes are dry. When it malfunctions, your dryer cycle either never ends or shuts off while clothes are still damp. Replacing one runs $180 to $280 in labor and parts.

The drum bearing comes next. It wears out faster in combo units because they cycle moisture and heat through the same drum used for washing. A failing bearing produces a grinding or squealing noise during the drying phase. This repair costs $300 to $450 since the entire drum assembly must come out.

The water inlet valve controls water flow into the wash chamber. Mineral deposits from Salt Lake City's hard water can clog these valves. Replacement typically costs $150 to $220. The drain pump, which must handle both wash and dry cycle moisture, fails around $200 to $350.

Control boards on combo units are expensive to diagnose and replace because they manage both wash and dry functions. If your machine won't start or cycles won't advance, the board is often the culprit. Budget $250 to $400 for diagnosis and replacement.

Salt Lake City's Hard Water and Combo Unit Longevity

Homes across Salt Lake City deal with mineral-heavy water that accelerates combo unit wear. The same hot water that leaves deposits on your shower glass deposits calcification inside inlet valves, fill tubes, and heating elements. Combo units, which repeat heating cycles in the same chassis, build up mineral scale faster than traditional machines.

In neighborhoods like Sugar House and the area near Liberty Park, many older Victorian homes have pipes that compound this issue. The older the plumbing infrastructure, the harder the water feels. We recommend descaling your combo unit's inlet valves and heating element every 18 months if you're in Salt Lake City proper.

Homes in the Millcreek Canyon foothills and East Bench areas see this problem intensified during winter. As mineral-rich snowmelt feeds the water system, mineral content spikes. Running a commercial descaling solution through a cleaning cycle costs under $30 and can prevent $300+ valve replacements.

When to Repair Versus Replace a Combo Unit

If your combo unit is under 5 years old and the repair quote is under $300, fixing it makes financial sense. The machine still has years of life left, and the service cost won't justify replacement.

Once your unit crosses the 7-year mark, do the math. A control board replacement ($300-$400) plus drum bearing work ($350) equals $650-$700. A new combo unit costs $800 to $1,500 installed. At that repair price, starting fresh with a new machine often wins.

Factor in your machine's repair history, too. If you've had the inlet valve replaced twice in three years and now the thermal sensor is failing, the unit is nickel-and-diming you. That's a sign the machine's overall reliability has declined, and replacement saves future headaches.

Can Standalone Units Replace Your Combo in Salt Lake City Homes?

The original appeal of a combo unit was space savings. Many Salt Lake City condos built in the 1990s-2000s have laundry closets barely 30 inches wide. Standard washer-dryer pairs need roughly 60 inches of width when stacked or placed side-by-side.

But compact washers and dryers do exist. You can now find stackable units that fit tighter spaces, and some models are only 24 inches wide. They won't match the capacity of your combo, and they cost a bit more individually, but paired repairs are often cheaper than one combo breakdown.

Speak with a technician about your home's layout before deciding. If you're in a Cottonwood Heights or Herriman home with a dedicated laundry room added during a renovation, standalone units might actually free up usable space compared to your current combo.

Maintenance Steps to Extend Combo Unit Life

Clean the door gasket monthly. Moisture and lint trapped in the rubber seal create mold and allow water to leak. A quick wipe with a damp cloth takes two minutes and prevents $200 in mold-related repairs.

Run the cleaning cycle on your machine every 30 washes. Most modern combo units have a self-clean mode designed to flush the drum and spray arms. Use a commercial washing machine cleaner, not bleach, which can damage internal seals over time.

Check the drain hose for kinks or blockages every three months. A kinked hose forces the pump to work harder, shortening its lifespan. In Salt Lake City's drier climate, lint accumulation happens faster here than in humid regions, so don't skip this step.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a washer dryer combo repair typically take?

Is there a warranty on combo unit repairs?

Most repair shops, including Eagle Appliance Repair, offer a 12-month parts warranty on replaced components. This means if that new thermal sensor fails again within a year due to defect, we replace it free. Labor warranties typically run 30-90 days.

Do I need a technician to diagnose my combo unit, or can I troubleshoot it myself?

You can check basic things like clearing the lint filter, inspecting the door seal, and running a cleaning cycle. Beyond that, combo units require testing equipment. A professional diagnosis typically costs $75-$100 but saves you from guessing and making the problem worse.

Why does my combo unit take forever to dry clothes?

The thermal sensor or heating element has likely failed. A clogged air vent duct also causes slow drying—check if your vent outlet on the exterior of your Salt Lake City home is blocked by lint or weather debris. If it's not the vent, schedule a service call.
