What Microwave Repairs Actually Cost in Lehi

A typical microwave repair in Lehi runs between $150 and $400, including the service call and parts. The largest variable is which component has failed. A magnetron replacement—the part that generates heat—usually costs $200–$350 in labor and materials combined. A capacitor or high-voltage diode repair is on the lower end, around $100–$200 total.

Service calls in Lehi average $60–$80, but most repair shops (including Eagle Appliance Repair) waive that fee if you proceed with the repair. Diagnostic time is built in at no charge when you commit to fixing the unit. Parts themselves range wildly: a $15 door latch costs less than a $120 magnetron, but labor is where the real expense sits.

Repair vs. Replacement: The 50% Rule

There's a simple threshold most appliance professionals use: if repair costs more than half the price of a new microwave, replacement wins. A basic new microwave costs $120–$250 at big-box retailers. If your repair quote exceeds $100–$125, you're in the replacement zone financially.

But cost isn't the only factor. A microwave older than 8 years has a higher chance of a second failure within 12 months. Even if one repair is affordable, you might spend $300 over the next two years chasing different problems. Newer units come with manufacturer warranties; older ones leave you fully exposed.

Common Microwave Failures and Their Price Tags

The magnetron is the most expensive repair because it's the heart of the unit. When it fails, the microwave won't heat anything—not a cup of coffee, not a frozen dinner. This repair runs $200–$350 and happens most often in microwaves 6+ years old due to normal wear on the filament inside.

A broken door latch is the cheapest fix. It's a plastic or metal switch that tells the microwave the door is closed. Replacement is $80–$150 total. High-voltage capacitors, thermal fuses, and turntable motors fall in the $100–$200 range. The turntable drive assembly—if the plate won't spin—usually costs $120–$180 to replace.

Red Flags That Point to Replacement

If your microwave is sparking inside, don't attempt a repair—sparking indicates waveguide damage or a foreign metal object lodged in the cavity. This repair runs $200–$400 and is rarely worth it on older units. Persistent burning smells also signal internal damage that's expensive to diagnose and fix.

Another signal: if you've already had one repair in the last 18 months, a second problem is likely brewing. The cost-benefit math shifts when you're stacking repairs. A single $180 repair on a 6-year-old microwave might be reasonable; a second $200 repair six months later tips the scale toward replacement.
