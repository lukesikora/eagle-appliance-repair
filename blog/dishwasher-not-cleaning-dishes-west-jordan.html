Why Your Dishwasher Is Running But Not Cleaning

A dishwasher that completes a cycle without cleaning dishes typically points to one of three culprits: water isn't reaching the dishes, the water isn't hot enough, or detergent isn't doing its job. The machine itself may be functioning mechanically, but one of these three elements has broken down.

Water temperature is a silent killer. Dishwasher detergent requires water at least 120 degrees Fahrenheit to dissolve and activate properly. If your home's water heater is set too low or if you have a long run of cold pipes from the heater to the kitchen, the water entering the dishwasher may be lukewarm. Lukewarm water means detergent sits on your dishes as a film rather than breaking down grease.

Check Your Dishwasher Filter First

The filter is your first line of defense. Located at the bottom of the wash chamber, it catches food particles, grease, and debris. When it clogs, water can't circulate properly, and cleaning performance drops fast.

Remove the lower rack and look at the cylindrical filter beneath it. Twist it counterclockwise to remove it. Rinse it under hot running water and use an old toothbrush to scrub away stuck-on food. If you see a lot of buildup, soak it in hot water with a tablespoon of baking soda for 15 minutes before scrubbing again. Reinstall it, run a short cycle, and check your next load.

This single step solves about 30% of the "not cleaning" complaints we receive. It takes five minutes and costs nothing.

Inspect Your Spray Arms for Blockages

Spray arms are the rotating parts that shoot water jets onto your dishes. Each arm has small nozzles drilled into it. When these nozzles clog with mineral deposits or food debris, water pressure drops and coverage is uneven.

Remove both the upper and lower racks. Look at the spray arms—usually plastic tubes with holes along the underside. Check each hole with a toothpick or thin wire. Mineral deposits in hard water areas like West Jordan can calcify inside these nozzles. If you see white or tan buildup, soak the arms in equal parts white vinegar and hot water for 30 minutes, then use a toothpick to clear each opening.

If the arms won't come out easily or if you're uncomfortable removing them, that's when a technician can help. A blocked spray arm often goes unnoticed because the machine still runs and drains normally.

Detergent Type and Water Hardness Issues

West Jordan sits in the heart of Utah's hard water zone. Hard water contains dissolved minerals—mainly calcium and magnesium—that interfere with detergent performance. If you're using a powdered or gel detergent, those formulas rely on softening agents that hard water overpowers.

Switch to a detergent pod or tablet designed for hard water. Pods dissolve more completely and often contain additional enzymes for tough stains. If you've always used the same brand and suddenly notice poor cleaning, your water hardness may have shifted, or your detergent formula may have changed.

Also check the fill line. Overfilling the detergent dispenser won't help—in fact, excess detergent can leave residue on dishes. Follow the manufacturer's recommendation for your water hardness level.

West Jordan Water and Climate Factors

West Jordan's hard water is notorious among homeowners. The area's mineral-rich groundwater means deposits build up faster in dishwasher components than in softer-water regions. Homes in neighborhoods like Redwood Valley and around the West Jordan High School area all experience the same mineral accumulation.

Additionally, Utah's dry climate means pipes are more exposed to temperature swings. If your dishwasher is installed in an exterior wall or a garage-adjacent space (common in West Jordan homes built in the 2000s), cold incoming water takes longer to reach the full 120 degrees. This lag is especially noticeable during winter months when outdoor temps drop below freezing.

We recommend running your kitchen sink tap for 20-30 seconds before starting a wash cycle to purge cold water from the line. This ensures hotter water enters the dishwasher from the start. It's a small habit that makes a measurable difference in cleaning results.

When DIY Cleaning Isn't Enough

If you've cleaned the filter, cleared the spray arm nozzles, switched detergent, and verified your water temperature is above 120 degrees—and your dishes still come out dirty—there's likely an internal component that needs professional attention.

Common internal issues include a faulty water inlet valve (not allowing enough water into the tub), a malfunctioning heating element (water isn't reaching proper temperature), or a circulation pump that's lost pressure. These problems require a technician's diagnostic tools and can't be spotted by visual inspection.

Repair costs in West Jordan typically range from $150 to $400 in labor, depending on the component and complexity. A water inlet valve replacement runs around $200-$300 all-in; a heating element replacement is often $250-$350. Most parts come with a 12-month warranty when installed by a certified technician.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I clean my dishwasher filter?

Inspect your filter monthly and clean it weekly if you have a large household or run multiple loads daily. In West Jordan homes where hard water and mineral content are high, weekly cleaning helps prevent mineral buildup inside the filter material itself. A clean filter is the easiest way to maintain consistent cleaning performance.

Can I use vinegar to clean the inside of my dishwasher?

Yes. Fill a dishwasher-safe bowl with white vinegar, place it in the upper rack, and run a hot cycle with no dishes inside. The vinegar dissolves mineral deposits on the interior walls and spray arms. Do this once a month in West Jordan's hard water environment to keep the machine fresh. Never use vinegar and baking soda together in the dishwasher—they'll neutralize each other.

Why does my dishwasher smell bad if it's not cleaning well?

Poor drainage or trapped food particles in the filter create an environment where bacteria and mold thrive. A clogged filter traps food that ferments and smells. This is another reason to inspect your filter regularly. If a smell persists after cleaning, the drain line may need attention from a professional.

Is it worth repairing a dishwasher that's 10 years old?

Most dishwashers last 10-15 years with proper maintenance. If your repair cost is under 50% of a new unit's price (new dishwashers run $400-$800 installed), repair makes financial sense. Factor in the age of your machine and the type of part failing. For older units in good condition otherwise, repair is often worth it.
